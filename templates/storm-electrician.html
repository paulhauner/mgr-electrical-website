Powerlines down? Stay at least 8 metres away and call Essential Energy on 13 20 80.

Lines in the street, the pole and the service line to your house belong to the network — an electrician can’t touch them until they’re made safe.

Trees on the house, roof damage or flooding: SES on 132 500. If anyone is in danger or there’s fire: 000 first.

After the storm

When the damage is on your side of the meter.

If the street has power and you don’t, or water has got into anything electrical, that’s Mac’s job.

Water in the switchboard or wiring

Rain through the roof, a flooded garage, water in light fittings. Stay clear, don’t reset anything, and ring. Wet wiring needs testing before it’s switched back on.

Power out — but only at your place

If the neighbours have power and you don’t, or a safety switch keeps tripping since the storm, the fault is usually inside your property.

Storm-damaged meter box, wiring or fittings

Damaged meter boxes, outdoor powerpoints, lights and cabling made safe first, then repaired properly and signed off.

Reports for your insurance claim

Mac can write up what was damaged, what caused it and what the repair involves, so you’ve got what the insurer asks for.

Before the storm

Get ready before the next one.

Storm season on the Coffs Coast runs through spring and summer. These are worth sorting while the sky’s still blue.

Surge protection

A surge protection device fitted at the switchboard helps protect TVs, computers, appliances and aircon from power surges during storms and when the power comes back on.

Generator inlet

A generator inlet and changeover switch lets you run the house — or the circuits that matter — from a portable generator during a long outage, safely and legally.

Safety switch check

Safety switches tested, and older boards upgraded so every circuit is protected. They’re what cuts the power fast when water gets where it shouldn’t.

Running a generator?

Never plug it into a powerpoint.

Back-feeding a generator through a powerpoint with a double-ended lead can send power back out into the street and kill a line worker fixing the outage. It can also damage your wiring and the generator.

The safe way is a generator inlet with a changeover switch, installed by a licensed electrician.

Storm emergency?

Don’t fill in a form — ring. Storm call-outs are taken day and night, and you’ll be told what you’re up for before work starts.
